# Next Qwen training study: evidence and proposed experiments

Research snapshot: 2026-09-11. **Planning only; this document does not authorize
job submission.** All training data and RL interaction must come from authorized
Fleet **training** tasks. External papers inform methods, never supply benchmark
content, solutions, or checkpoint-selection signals. Keep GLM out of this study.

## Recommendation

Continue teacher SFT, but make the next experiment a controlled comparison with
verified Qwen self-SFT, followed by a small SFT→RL experiment. Do not jump to many
epochs or a large RL run. The objective is higher held-out blackbox task success
at a matched inference budget, **not the largest training-loss decrease**.

First evaluate the existing dense-teacher V5 checkpoint: the
[repository audit](TRAINING_SEARCH_READINESS_2026-09-11.md#historical-qwen-studies-do-not-conflate-them)
found a roughly 48% fixed held-out loss reduction at LR `3e-6`, but no established
task-solving uplift. Preserve that setting as the new study's control; the older
small training-only loss reduction is not our latest learning evidence.

There is no established optimal Fleet recipe for exact Qwen3.8-27B in the sources
reviewed here. Its [official repository](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8#finetuning)
lists supported finetuning frameworks but does not prescribe a cyber learning
rate, batch size or epoch count. Older Qwen/QwQ studies provide useful starting
points, not Qwen3.8 or cyber-transfer guarantees.

## What teacher SFT, self-SFT and RL actually test

| Intervention | What it learns from | Question tested | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher SFT | Verified successful actions from stronger models on Fleet train tasks | Can Qwen acquire useful behavior that its current policy does not reliably produce? | Imitating teacher style, long detours or actions supported by context/tools unavailable to Qwen |
| Qwen self-SFT / rejection-sampling finetuning | Verified successes generated by a frozen, identified Qwen checkpoint | Can successful behavior already reachable by Qwen become more reliable? | Oversampling easy tasks and reinforcing accidental success or repetitive behavior |
| Online RL | New Qwen actions in isolated Fleet train environments, evaluated by the pinned verifier | Can outcome feedback improve action selection and recovery under Qwen's own state distribution? | Sparse rewards, truncated episodes, wrong grading, unstable/off-policy updates and expensive environment interaction |

Both SFT arms use assistant-token next-token cross-entropy conditioned on the
recorded history. They do **not** compare an independently generated student
rollout against a teacher rollout, and they are not teacher-logit KL distillation.
Tool results are observed context, not prediction targets. Verified self-SFT
uses reward to select examples but still optimizes a supervised objective; it
is not the same algorithm as PPO/GRPO. This is the filtered-behavior-cloning
setup explicitly described in [SWE-Gym §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v1#S3.SS1).

**Neither source is universally better.** Teacher data can cover train-task
families where Qwen has no successes; self data can better match the student's
reachable behavior. Keep these two questions separate:

- **Controlled source comparison:** teacher versus self on the same task-family
  intersection, same budget, masks, harness and recipe.
- **Best practical training system:** allow broader teacher coverage and
  separately tune each arm with the same search budget. Report the coverage
  difference instead of attributing everything to “teacher versus self.”

SFT need not cap a specialist at the teacher's aggregate performance: combining
demonstrations and domain specialization may help. But surpassing a frontier
model is an empirical claim requiring a fresh matched evaluation, not a
consequence of doing distillation. RL likewise is not guaranteed to discover
behaviors for which the policy never obtains useful feedback.

## What the primary literature actually reports

GBS means global examples/sequences per optimizer update as reported by that
source; it is **not automatically equivalent to Fleet packed windows or tokens**.
“Not specified” means the cited section does not establish that field.

Initial corpus: 491 successful GPT-4o/Claude trajectories. | Actual long-horizon software-agent precedent. Its hinted generation and filtering are interventions beyond merely keeping all existing successes. Python math/code reasoning, not cyber episodes. |
| [ReTool, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11536v1#S3.SS1); Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct | Cold-start SFT then PPO; RL AdamW LR `1e-6`, reported minibatch 512, maximum sequence 16,384, KL coefficient 0.0, 400 RL steps. Adapter choice and complete SFT recipe are not established by that section. | Outcome-reward tool RL improved mathematical reasoning. Interpreter outputs are masked. The paper's zero-KL choice, scale and short interpreter episodes are not automatically appropriate for Fleet. |
| [Post-Training Science for SFT, §2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01244v1#S2.SS1); dense Qwen3 0.6–32B and Llama, plus MoE follow-ups | 5,000 examples, one epoch, assistant-only labels, cosine. Reported optima near `3e-5` FullFT and `1e-3` LoRA. | Recent preprint on four anonymized customer datasets, not Fleet or Qwen3.8. Useful search bounds, not a transferable optimum. |

Two further results affect sequencing:

- [DeepSeek-R1 §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1#S2.SS4) used 800,000
  curated samples to distill into Qwen2.5/Llama students with SFT, without student
  RL. This supports teacher SFT as a real capability-transfer mechanism, but not
  an expected gain from a much smaller Fleet corpus. The paper's two-epoch
  statement in §2.3.3 concerns DeepSeek-V3-Base; it should not silently become an
  asserted two-epoch recipe for every distilled student.
- [WebDancer §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22648v1#S5) reports that cold-start
  SFT was important for tool-agent RL; subsequent RL helped Qwen2.5 non-reasoning
  models more than QwQ's aggregate pass rates. It also reports more invalid
  trajectories when long reasoning patterns transfer poorly. This argues for
  checking actual episode validity and reward density, not assuming longer
  reasoning or RL must help. Its recipe is information seeking, not exploitation.

Avoid the slogan “SFT memorizes, RL generalizes.” The
[comparative study with that title](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17161v1) found
that pattern on arithmetic-rule and visual-navigation shifts. A
[later controlled study](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00237v1) found prompt
diversity and suitable supervision could make SFT match or surpass RL on its
decision-making benchmarks. These are conditional experimental findings, not a
general theorem selecting the Fleet training method.

## Windows, samples, batches and weights

These are proposed accounting requirements, not a claim that every option is
already implemented. See [the scientific protocol](SCIENTIFIC_PROTOCOL.md).

1. **Task family** is the split/independence unit. A new version or sibling
   projection must not leak its family's solution into another split. Keep
   blackbox exploitation distinct from whitebox patching. Application-sharing
   train/dev splits are not application-held-out generalization.
2. **Trajectory** is a complete recorded episode. One verified success can
   contain many assistant actions plus tool observations; “one trace” is not
   “one training sample.” Keep teacher model and student checkpoint identities.
3. **Training window** is a context-length-bounded representation used by the
   trainer. Packing joins eligible segments into token-efficient windows;
   windowing can also preserve context for later targets. Neither invents new
   independent task evidence. Overlap used as context must be loss-masked, not
   counted as another copy of the same supervised target.
4. **Supervised tokens** are the assistant targets that actually enter loss.
   Mask user/system/tool observations, padding and duplicated context. Preserve
   real tool-error observations and subsequent recovery; do not delete them
   simply because the command failed. Do not reconstruct hidden teacher reasoning
   or teach privileged verifier answers as if the policy observed them.
5. **Effective batch** is microbatch × data-parallel replicas × accumulation.
   Tensor/pipeline-parallel ranks do not multiply independent examples. With
   variable label counts, log supervised tokens/update as well as windows/update.
   State the remainder/drop/padding policy before computing steps per epoch.
6. **Loss weighting** is a scientific choice. Global token mean is summed
   negative log-likelihood divided by summed supervised tokens; averaging each
   window's or GPU's mean is different when label counts differ. Verify
   distributed accumulation matches the declared objective. Token weighting
   gives long traces more influence; family-balanced weighting answers a
   different question and needs explicit implementation/testing.

For the source comparison, freeze the same eligible families, per-family trace
cap, sampling seed, total supervised-token exposure and validation targets.
Deduplicate repeated trajectories and report covered families, success-selection
rate, exclusions, target coverage and exposure per family. Do not equate equal
window counts with equal training budgets. Start with a predeclared cap of one
or two distinct verified successes per family, then test broader coverage before
adding many near-duplicate successes. This cap is a proposed Fleet setting, not
an established optimum.

## A bounded next-run plan

This is a proposal for approval, **not a sweep that should be submitted now**.
Every new/changed executable job configuration first needs the dev-cluster
qualification required by [cluster policy](CLUSTER_ALERTS_AND_INFERENCE_SERVING.md).
CPU data work should use the data cluster when its access and storage contracts
are verified. Neither site is assumed to share production secrets or files.

### 1. Freeze the study before training

Use one exact Qwen3.8-27B checkpoint for every starting arm. Build a fresh,
versioned Fleet train/dev/test manifest **before** selecting traces or windows;
never silently change the previous split. If retaining the 20-task dev set,
require 20 distinct excluded families and recognize that it is a coarse tuning
instrument: repeated rollouts and thousands of windows do not create thousands
of independent test tasks. Reserve a separate untouched final Fleet test set.
If enough genuinely new families exist, enlarge dev/test prospectively.

Primary metric: mean held-out task success at a fixed pass@1 inference budget.
Also report pass@4, report-submission/validity rate, tokens/time and paired
family-level uncertainty. Frontier comparisons need the same tasks, tools,
verifier and stated resource budget; a stronger model's published benchmark
number is not the baseline. External evaluation stays sealed until final recipe
and checkpoint selection are frozen.

### 2. Compare and tune SFT without a giant Cartesian sweep

Use full-parameter SFT first because the repository already has the strongest
Qwen operational evidence for it. Keep AdamW and the established precision,
masking, tokenizer and tested context implementation fixed.

- Screen **teacher and self arms × LR `{3e-6, 1e-5, 3e-5}`**, six candidates,
  at GBS 8 and one fixed seed. The low value preserves our demonstrated control, the
  middle is near START's order of magnitude, and the high tests the newer
  Qwen3 FullFT result. These are **search points, not a guarantee of stability
  or optimality**. Qualify each changed config on dev before a longer run.
  Keep the currently implemented constant schedule fixed in this first screen;
  a cosine/warmup comparison requires small explicit recipe support and new
  tests/qualification, not an undocumented native override.
- Start with a fixed quarter-epoch target budget to reject numerical/runtime
  defects, not to declare a capability winner. Advance at most two candidates
  per arm to one full epoch, retaining the original schedule horizon rather
  than silently restarting a new LR schedule. A high-LR-only early winner may
  reverse later, so use common-budget comparisons.
- At the best LR for each arm, compare GBS 8 versus 32, initially one epoch.
  Keep total supervised-token exposure fixed and report changed update count
  and wall time. Smaller batch is not “worse utilization” by definition, and
  larger batch is not automatically better learning.
- Inspect checkpoints at approximately 0.5, 1 and 2 epochs; test a third epoch
  only if Fleet dev outcomes justify it. For an exact comparison of different
  cosine horizons, use separately frozen schedules; an intermediate checkpoint
  of a two-epoch schedule is not a one-epoch cosine run.
- If teacher and self offer complementary family coverage, add one predeclared
  50/50 **supervised-token** mixture at the selected recipe. It is another arm,
  not a silent replacement of either dataset. Repeat the final contenders with
  at least two training seeds before claiming a small improvement.

The LR/schedule/epoch prior is deliberately cautious: the recent
[SFT sweep's epoch section](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01244v1#S7) finds validation
loss overfitting beyond roughly two epochs in its settings, while downstream
judgments need not track that loss and instruction-following can erode. That is
reason to measure both, not to impose a universal two-epoch ceiling.

If FullFT cost becomes the bottleneck, add a **separate** LoRA experiment after
exact-Qwen module coverage/export qualification: rank 32/64, alpha32,
all-linear coverage, LR `{1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3}`, GBS 8/16. Do not launch this extra
grid concurrently with the initial full-SFT question. Adapter LRs and target
modules need their own validation; the literature does not justify silently
substituting a cheap LoRA result for FullFT.

### 3. Measure cheaply; select on actual Fleet behavior

Run the same complete dev-loss calculation before training and about 4 times
per epoch initially. Log token-weighted NLL and task-macro NLL separately.
Pilot its overhead; if it exceeds roughly 5–10% of training time, reduce the
frequency, not the dev membership or target masks. Those frequency/overhead
numbers are proposed operational choices, not paper-derived optima.

Evaluate actual dev task outcomes only for the small shortlist and selected
epoch checkpoints. Keep validation targets common across teacher/self arms:
loss on each arm's own stylistically different corpus is not an apples-to-apples
comparison. A modest train-loss reduction alone is neither a training defect
nor evidence of improved exploitation.

W&B should group by study/split hash, source arm, recipe and seed. Record
optimizer step, cumulative supervised tokens, LR, loss, dev metrics, gradient
norm, useful throughput, checkpoint identity and infrastructure-invalid counts.
Never upload task content, private trajectories, flags or credentials. Exact
immutable manifests and native checkpoints remain authoritative; dashboards are
an index, not proof of acceptance. Do not use a W&B agent to blindly launch an
unbounded sweep through the Jobs API.

### 4. Then test SFT→RL against a real SFT baseline

First qualify one backend on dev with exact Fleet reward acquisition, a real
parameter update, checkpoint save and recovery. Miles versus SkyRL is a systems
choice at this stage; testing both simultaneously confounds a learning study.
Begin from the better dev-selected SFT checkpoint, with a frozen Qwen base arm
only if needed to ask whether the SFT warm start helps.

As an initial engineering experiment, consider 4–8 completions per training
prompt and a small 8–16-prompt batch, then tune after measuring episode duration
and reward variance. Distinguish prompt batch, rollout group and optimizer
minibatch. Measure how many groups have differing **valid** rewards; all-equal
groups provide no within-group GRPO advantage. Do not replace missing rewards
or truncated episodes with genuine failure labels or endlessly refill them.
Use the trusted Fleet verifier, not a newly invented reward trained from test
outcomes. Keep reward normalization, clipping and KL policy explicit rather than
copying ReTool's zero-KL setting by habit.

Scale only after the short run shows usable reward signal and operational
recovery. Continue if matched Fleet dev success improves without rising invalid
episodes or collapsed behavior; stop and diagnose otherwise. A specialized
Qwen surpassing frontier models remains a hypothesis until the final untouched
test demonstrates it under the frozen protocol.
